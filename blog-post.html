Inside the Sunset Session Vol. 1 mix

Every mix starts the same way: a folder of tracks that don't belong together yet. Sunset Session Vol. 1 began as thirty afro house and melodic house records collected over a few months of digging, most of them recorded live at soundchecks or picked up from other DJs' sets.

The goal was never to make a technically perfect mix — it was to capture the feeling of a sunset set at Watergate, where the room slowly shifts from daylight energy into something warmer and more hypnotic.

Finding the arc

The first hour leans on rolling, percussive afro house — tracks built around live drums and vocal chops. From there the mix opens up into longer, more melodic arrangements, letting pads and strings carry the room rather than the kick.

"I wanted the mix to feel like the last two hours of a really good night — not the peak, but everything right after it."

Technically, that meant a lot of manual EQ work between transitions rather than relying on autosync. Half the mix was recorded in a single take, the other half was rebuilt in the studio from smaller loops that worked in the room but not on record.

What's next

Sunset Session Vol. 2 is already in progress, recorded this time on the beach in Cape Town. Expect it before the end of the year, alongside a handful of new dates across Europe and South Africa.
